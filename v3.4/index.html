04 Independent category strategy case · Nov 2026

Myprotein | The Texture Hypothesis

Self-initiated cross-market category research
Not commissioned, sponsored or endorsed by THG or Myprotein

Explore the full interactive case study ↗
Core capabilities
Category & product strategyCross-market desk researchCommercial test design
Main contribution

Independently framed an untested texture-led protein proposition, compared a purposive set of 17 Korean and UK product references, and developed a staged evidence plan for a potential UK pilot.

What I specifically did
  • Reviewed a selected register of 10 Korean and seven UK protein products, keeping variant-level evidence separate from broader product-family descriptions.
  • Used public THG disclosures to frame the business context without treating segment revenue or branded unit growth as evidence of UK demand for a new texture.
  • Proposed a matched smooth-base versus inclusion-added sensory test, followed by paid-trial, repeat-demand and contribution-margin checks.
Analytical judgement

A product difference is not proven demand. Korean texture references make a UK proposition testable, but a selected product comparison cannot establish consumer preference, incremental demand or commercial viability.

Independent hypothesis and research design, not a validated market result.
Output / observed outcome

Published an independent interactive category case study with product evidence, explicit coverage limitations, falsifiable hypotheses and illustrative commercial sensitivity checks.

Evidence scope & limitations

No UK consumer test, commissioned brand project, private THG data, product launch, verified sales uplift or company endorsement. Financial scenarios are illustrative, not forecasts.

05 Independent market portfolio · Oct 2026

Lidl GB

Self-initiated Power BI analysis
Not employment or commissioned brand work

See original Lidl portfolio ↗
Core capabilities
Market & competitor analysisKPI selectionPower BI / DAX
Main contribution

Independently selected analytical questions and KPI comparisons, then built a Power BI dashboard to examine Lidl’s position in the UK grocery market.

What I specifically did
  • Used publicly available UK grocery-market evidence with Excel, Power Query, Power BI and DAX.
  • Compared Lidl with Tesco, Sainsbury’s and Aldi, treating market share, competitor gaps, switching indicators and planned expansion as different signals.
  • Used a retailer-share snapshot for the 12 weeks ending 17 May 2026 and checked reporting periods and market definitions before comparison.
  • Distinguished percentage-point share differences from relative percentage growth.
Analytical judgement

Share is not the same as cause. A point-in-time share comparison does not on its own explain switching behaviour or the effect of proposed store openings. I kept these questions separate rather than presenting one metric as a growth explanation.

Reasoning from the analysis design, not a verified Lidl business outcome.
Possible next analysis · not completed

Match retailer share with switching evidence for comparable periods before drawing a conclusion about growth drivers or store expansion.

Output / observed outcome

Completed an independently designed analytical dashboard and comparison framework.

Evidence scope & limitations

No internal Lidl data, client engagement, implemented recommendation or verified sales uplift.
