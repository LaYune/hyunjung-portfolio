04 Independent market portfolio · Oct 2026

Lidl GB

Self-initiated Power BI analysis
Not employment or commissioned brand work

See original Lidl portfolio ↗
Core capabilities
Market & competitor analysisKPI selectionPower BI / DAX
Main contribution

Independently selected analytical questions and KPI comparisons, then built a Power BI dashboard to examine Lidl’s position in the UK grocery market.

What I specifically did
  • Used publicly available UK grocery-market evidence with Excel, Power Query, Power BI and DAX.
  • Compared Lidl with Tesco, Sainsbury’s and Aldi, treating market share, competitor gaps, switching indicators and planned expansion as different signals.
  • Used a retailer-share snapshot for the 12 weeks ending 17 May 2026 and checked reporting periods and market definitions before comparison.
  • Distinguished percentage-point share differences from relative percentage growth.
Analytical judgement

Share is not the same as cause. A point-in-time share comparison does not on its own explain switching behaviour or the effect of proposed store openings. I kept these questions separate rather than presenting one metric as a growth explanation.

Reasoning from the analysis design, not a verified Lidl business outcome.
Output / observed outcome

Completed an independently designed analytical dashboard and comparison framework.

Evidence boundary: No internal Lidl data, client engagement, implemented recommendation or verified sales uplift.

05 Independent market portfolio · Oct 2026

FitFlop UK

Self-initiated Power BI analysis
Not employment or commissioned brand work

See original FitFlop portfolio ↗
Core capabilities
Brand & competitor analysisSearch-interest interpretationData storytelling
Main contribution

Designed two Power BI pages examining public product/pricing information alongside five-year UK Google Trends relative search-interest indices across competitors.

What I specifically did
  • Selected the comparison questions, measures and narrative emphasis independently.
  • Structured Page 1 around public product/pricing comparisons and Page 2 around UK competitor brand-search interest over the same five-year Google Trends window.
  • Treated Google Trends values as relative indices (0–100), not sales or customer volumes; documented the portfolio’s use of 0.5 when the source reported “<1”.
Analytical judgement

Search visibility is not sales. Public price/product differences and relative search-interest indices can frame a positioning question, but they cannot establish conversion, revenue or why consumers prefer a brand.

A defensible interpretation limit; no unverified competitor-performance finding added.
Output / observed outcome

Two-page independent Power BI portfolio presenting the comparisons and their interpretive limits.

Evidence boundary: No private company data, validated brand sales numbers, conversion figures or proven commercial outcome.

Original project evidence: Use the portfolio links in each case. The V2 interactive retail dashboard is a separate illustration with hypothetical values, not a reproduction of Lidl’s source dataset. No unverified original chart is embedded here.
