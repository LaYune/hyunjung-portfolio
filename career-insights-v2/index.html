Two signals, different meanings.

Reviewing observable product and pricing structure alongside relative branded-search interest without mistaking attention for sales.

Business question
What can publicly visible product, price and search information suggest about FitFlop's competitive position?
Evidence & method
Personally built two Power BI pages comparing publicly visible UK product/price information and Google Trends branded-search indices against competitors.
Analytical judgement
Kept assortment and price observations distinct from relative search visibility, since they describe different behaviours.
Decision use
Use the contrast to form hypotheses about competitive positioning and identify which internal sales or customer evidence would be needed to test them.
Evidence boundary
Independent study, not FitFlop client work. Search indices do not establish revenue, conversions, market share or causal effects.

Way of working

From market signal
to decision support.

Evidence → Diagnosis → Pattern → Insight → Recommendation → Communication → Follow-up, where actual work supports each stage.

01

Frame the decision.

Start with the question and the commercial context. Choose comparisons that could genuinely help a stakeholder decide what to investigate.

02

Interrogate the signal.

Examine definitions, comparable time windows, the difference between branded search and sales, and what is observation versus speculation.

03

Recommend the next step.

Explain the implication and evidence gap. Where a stakeholder discussion occurred, identify it precisely; where impact is unmeasured, say so.
